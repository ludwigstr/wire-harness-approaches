Abstract

The increasing automation of wire harness manufacturing creates a growing demand for models that accurately predict the behavior of flexible wires during manipulation. Research in Physical AI is advancing world models that enable autonomous agents to predict the consequences of their actions, yet remains largely disconnected from deformable linear object (DLO) modeling. DLO research has long built dynamics models for diverse wire control tasks without framing them as world models, while world model research has turned to deformable objects but has so far largely overlooked wires.

This paper adopts a functional definition of world models to bridge the gap between DLO dynamics modeling and world model research, benchmarking representative approaches from both fields on a near-industry wire handling task. The benchmark evaluates how world and dynamic models represent and predict DLO motion, particularly how the physical properties like stiffness and rest shape, that have a strong influence on the accuracy of DLO prediction, are introduced. The benchmark establishes a common evaluation framework and provides adoption guidance for comparing physics-based, world-model, and dynamic-model approaches for DLO prediction.

Pipeline Overview

Perception & DLO Tracking

All approaches share a common perception front-end that extracts the wire harness state from overhead camera recordings of real manipulation sequences:

  1. Segmentation — instance masks for each of the 4 cables using a fine-tuned FastDLO model
  2. Centerline extraction — per-cable skeleton from the segmentation mask
  3. Node tracking — 16 equidistant nodes resampled along each centerline, tracked across frames to provide ground-truth positions
  4. Connector tracking — hub and plug positions measured from fiducial markers, providing the boundary conditions (actions) for every approach

Figure: Perception pipeline — camera frame → segmentation → centerline → tracked nodes

Benchmark Setup

Each approach receives the same inputs: the initial cable configuration (node positions at frame 0) and the measured connector trajectories. The task is to predict the 14 interior node positions per cable at every subsequent frame. Both end nodes (hub connector and plug connector) are pinned to their measured tracks — they serve as the driving boundary condition, not a prediction target.

The benchmark spans 36 manipulation clips recorded on a real wire harness assembly board, with a variety of connector motions (insertions, retractions, lateral sweeps). Each clip contains 4 cables with 16 tracked nodes, yielding 2,304 predicted node trajectories in total.

Benchmark Approaches

10 approaches across three paradigms: physics engines, world models, and learned dynamics models.

Physics Engines

MuJoCo 10.9 mm/node

DER cable composite with CMA-ES parameter fitting. 16 nodes, ball joints with per-joint stiffness.

Running gen 20
Genesis 12.7 mm/node

PD-controlled Cosserat rod with GPU-accelerated differentiable physics.

Running gen 29
Newton 8.1 mm/node

Lumped-parameter DER model. Per-joint bending and twisting with CMA-ES optimization.

Running gen 12
DLO-Lab 17.0 mm/node

MPM-based Cosserat rod with stretch and bending scaling factors.

Running gen 19

World Models

PhysWorld 4.5 mm/node

GNN world model with MPM-fitted particle fields. One GNN trained on all 36 clips.

Best overall
RopeDreamer 18.3 mm/node

Dreamer RSSM with quaternion chain state. Pretrained on X-Planar clips, fine-tuned on eval data.

PhysTwin 22.0 mm/node

Mass-spring digital twin with per-clip spring fitting via differentiable simulation.

Learned Dynamics Models

Yang et al. 13.3 mm/node

IN-biLSTM graph network. Interaction network with bidirectional LSTM along the chain.

Caporali et al. 15.8 mm/node

MLP dynamics model trained on MuJoCo transitions with real fine-tuning.

AdaptiGraph 29.4 mm/node

Material-adaptive GNN. Sim-pretrained with real adaptation. (45.5 sim-only)
