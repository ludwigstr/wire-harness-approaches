A benchmark of 10 physics-based and learned approaches for predicting wire harness dynamics in manufacturing, evaluated on 36 real manipulation clips with 4 cables and 16 tracked nodes each.

Abstract

The increasing automation of wire harness manufacturing creates a growing demand for models that accurately predict the behavior of flexible wires during manipulation. Research in Physical AI is advancing world models that enable autonomous agents to predict the consequences of their actions, yet remains largely disconnected from deformable linear object (DLO) modeling. DLO research has long built dynamics models for diverse wire control tasks without framing them as world models, while world model research has turned to deformable objects but has so far largely overlooked wires.

This paper adopts a functional definition of world models to bridge the gap between DLO dynamics modeling and world model research, benchmarking representative approaches from both fields on a near-industry wire handling task. The benchmark evaluates how world and dynamic models represent and predict DLO motion, particularly how the physical properties like stiffness and rest shape, that have a strong influence on the accuracy of DLO prediction, are introduced. The benchmark establishes a common evaluation framework and provides adoption guidance for comparing physics-based, world-model, and dynamic-model approaches for DLO prediction.

Key observation. In every physics engine, the single most effective fitted parameter is a per-joint rest angle: the cable's plastic set. A cable on the board does not relax to a straight line, and approaches that model this rest shape gain the most.

Pipeline Overview

Perception & DLO Tracking

All approaches share one perception front-end that turns overhead camera recordings of real manipulation sequences into cable skeletons. Click a step for its details.

Segmentation: class map per frame

The segmentation pipeline exports, for every processed frame, the raw camera image (2000 × 2660 px) and a class map that labels cable pixels (yellow) and mover pixels (green). The tracker reads only these two images, so it needs no source video.

  • The longest run of consecutive source frames is kept and duplicates are dropped, so every step is exactly one source frame.
  • Frames are sampled at 4 fps (0.25 s per step).

Board and module detection

  • find_board locates the board bezel and gives the pixel-to-millimetre transform, so all tracks are in metres on the board plane.
  • track_harness finds the five modules per frame from the class map, the hub and the four plugs on their movers, with stable identities and the hub first.
  • The hub and plug positions are the boundary conditions (actions) handed to every approach.

Cable routes (final_v1 tracking, 1 Nov 2026)

  1. Per-frame detector (tracking_v2.py): generates diverse route candidates, uses one global length per cable, and runs Viterbi over the whole clip.
  2. TrackDLO-style tracker (track_hybrid.py): CPD/GMM with a motion-coherence prior, all four cables in one model, followed frame to frame. A chain is reset to the detector's route when it leaves the evidence or when its ends jump by more than 60 mm (recording cuts).

Thin-wire re-trace of cable 3 (magenta)

Cable 3 is a loose bundle of 2–3 thin wires that the class map misses over long stretches. track_pink.py traces it directly in the camera image:

  • thin dark lines are found with a black-hat filter;
  • the route is the cheapest path from the hub to its mover, with extra cost on the three thick cables and a pull towards the previous frame.

16 equidistant nodes per cable

Each cable is traced from the hub to its mover and resampled to 16 nodes. One clip is stored as an npz with c0..c3 of shape (T, 16, 2) in metres, node 0 at the hub, plus fps, source_frames, a per-frame reset flag and the magenta cable length.

Benchmark Setup

Each approach receives the same inputs: the initial cable configuration (node positions at frame 0) and the measured connector trajectories. The task is to predict the cable shape at every subsequent frame. Both end nodes (hub connector and plug connector) follow their measured tracks: they are the driving boundary condition, not a prediction target.

The benchmark spans 36 manipulation clips recorded on a real wire harness assembly board (27 on 21 Oct, 3 on 26 Oct and 6 on 28 Oct 2026), whole clips with no trims, 118–181 frames each and 5,853 frames in total. Each clip contains 4 cables with 16 tracked nodes.

Metric: mean node error (mm/node)

Pipeline

  1. Twin fit (physworld_mpm_fit.py, elastoplastic, NVIDIA Warp). Global stage: homogeneous Young's modulus E, density ρ, board friction μ, Poisson ratio ν and yield stress. Local stage: per-particle E, ρ and μ on about 23k particles per clip; ν and yield stay global. Loss as in the paper: Chamfer + 0.1 × smooth-L1 track loss over all frames.
  2. Demonstrations (physworld_mpm_demos.py --n 40): 40 demos per clip generated from the twin.
  3. GNN world model (physworld_mpm_gnn.py): trained on the demos with the multi-step loss of Eq. 8, noise on the history and on Φ.
  4. Φ fine-tuning: per-node (log E, log ρ), 2 × 16 × 4 = 128 values per clip, fitted on the real clip with the network frozen. Objective: open-loop rollout MSE + Chamfer; Φ stays within ±1.5 (log) of the twin's value.
  5. Evaluation: 5-frame warm-up, then open loop from the measured end motions, ends pinned.

Deviation from the paper

  • On the 36 clips one pooled GNN is trained on the demos of all clips; the paper trains one per scene. Φ is still fitted per clip.

Pipeline (as in the paper: simulation only)

  1. Twins (ropedreamer_twins14.py): every clip is replayed in the MuJoCo general-fit environment with the real mover paths and timing.
  2. Variations (yang_make_dataset.py): speed-only motion variation (one shared time warp) and domain randomisation around the fit:
    • bend and twist ×0.5–2, per-cable jitter ×0.85–1.18
    • damping ×1/3–3, mass ×0.7–1.4 (clipped to 0.01–0.5 kg/m), μ ×0.5–2
    • rest angles ×0.8–1.2
    Rollouts that add mover–cable contacts not present in the real recording are rejected: 5,173 rollouts kept (557 rejected), 219k frames, 6.1 h on 16 workers.
  3. Training with the paper's settings: β = 1, lr 1e-4, batch 32, paper loss only; 10 % rollout-level validation, early stopping after 10 epochs; tracking-noise augmentation calibrated on the real tracks. Stopped at epoch 106 after 5.7 h.
  4. Evaluation: 5-frame warm-up, open loop, both end nodes pinned.

Deviations from the paper

  • Two driven ends instead of one grasp.
  • Quaternion loss up to sign; ELBO as a sum over z-standardised dimensions; free nats.

ropedreamer_twins14/, approaches/08_ropedreamer.py

A planar spring-mass digital twin fitted to each clip's video, then re-simulated from frame 0 with the measured end motions.

36-clip result

The twin

  • Mass points: the 16 tracked nodes of each cable, 2 g each, not fitted.
  • Springs: chain neighbours + node pairs closer than a fitted radius in the first frame (at most 6 per node). They also carry bending, because a spring-mass model has no bending angle.
  • Forces: springs, dashpots on connected pairs, drag, Coulomb board friction. The two end nodes move kinematically on their tracks; 1 ms steps.

Fit, per clip (the four cables share damping, drag and friction)

  1. Stage 1, CMA-ES on homogeneous k, dashpot γ, drag δ, friction μ and radius. Cost: mean node error of the full re-simulation from frame 0.
  2. Stage 2, Adam through the simulator on a stiffness per spring (+ γ, δ, μ) on random 8-frame windows. Every 10 iterations the full re-simulation is scored and the best iterate is kept, because window-only optimisation diverged on long clips.

Not implemented from the paper

  • Gaussian-splat rendering (node tracks are compared, not images), 3-D, and collisions.

Pipeline

  1. Identification (yang_identify.py --engine newton): differential evolution on the one-step error (Eq. 1) over k_stretch, d_stretch, k_bend, d_bend, k_twist, d_twist, mass per metre and μ, on all evaluation clips.
  2. Simulator control: the identified simulator alone on the clips.
  3. Synthetic data: real mover paths with speed-only variation ×0.65–1.4 (one shared time warp), parameters randomised around the identified set. Built from the 14 clips: 1,305 rollouts (95 rejected), 246k transitions.
  4. Training: IN + bi-LSTM, validation split per rollout. A transition is dropped only if its stretch gap exceeds that of its clip's real-timing rollout by more than 10 mm, so violations the real recording already has are tolerated.
  5. Evaluation: 5-frame warm-up, open loop, ends pinned.

Identified parameters (X-Planar run)

DE over 40 generations and 4,920 evaluations, one-step error 3.98 mm/node, all values inside the bounds:

k_stretch 4367 N/md_stretch 0.016 k_bend 3.66 N·m/radd_bend 7.45 N·m·s/rad k_twist 0.053 N·m/radd_twist 0.14 mass 0.087 kg/mμ 4.48

Additions to the paper

  • Domain randomisation, speed-only motion variation, and the tolerant stretch-gap filter. Without the filter, the soft identified stretch dropped 71.9 % of the transitions; with it, 93.9 % are kept.

Pipeline

  1. Synthetic data (make_dataset_real.py --mode step --stride 1): every chain starts from a real tracked cable state; the ends follow the real end paths with speed-only variation ×0.8–1.2. One transition is one video frame: 0.15 s of end motion plus 0.1 s of settling.
    • Sampled: m 0.02–0.10 kg, k_b 0.2–3.0 N·m/rad, k_d 5–40 N·s/m, k_c 0.05–0.4 N/node
    • Rest shape: frame-0 turning angles ×0.6–1.2
  2. Training: the paper's Fig. 3 FiLM MLP, 25 epochs (AdamW + OneCycle, MSE on node positions). On the 36 clips the training data is built from the 36 clips.
  3. Online estimation: m, k_b, k_d per cable start at 0.04 / 1.0 / 20; each frame is forecast before it is used for one Adam step on the parameters, so the score never sees the frame it predicts. The rollout is open loop from frame 0 with the measured ends.

caporali_et_al/, approaches/09_caporali.py

A material-conditioned graph-based neural dynamics model (GBND) with one physical property variable φ, estimated few-shot on each real cable.

Adapted on real clip

Model
GBND, 0.51 M weights, 4 rounds of message passing
Scored
16 nodes, ends pinned, frames 5…T−1
36-clip result
29.42 mean / 27.83 median

The graph

  • Vertices: the 16 cable nodes with a 3-frame history as displacements (translation-equivariant), particle type (object / end effector), material, and φ.
  • Edges: chain neighbours + all pairs within 12 cm.
  • Decoder: every node's displacement; the two driven end nodes are set by the action.

Pipeline

  1. Simulator data (make_dataset_real.py --mode seq): every chain starts from a real tracked frame; the ends follow the real end path with speed-only variation ×0.8–1.2. One transition is one 0.25 s frame with the 4 preceding frames as history. Same parameter ranges as Caporali.
  2. Simulation training: 20 epochs, one-step MSE (best validation one-step error 1.26 mm on the 14-clip data).
  3. Real fine-tuning (not in the paper): the real frame transitions, 60 epochs, φ fixed at 0.5 because the real stiffness is unknown.
  4. Evaluation: φ per cable from the first 5 frames (the paper uses Bayesian optimisation; for one scalar a dense grid is equivalent), then open loop with the model's own history and the measured ends.
